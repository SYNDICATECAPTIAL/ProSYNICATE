What This Indicator Does

Fakeout Pro detects high-probability reversal setups based on the concept of liquidity sweeps — moments when price temporarily breaks a key structural level (sweeping stop orders), then closes back inside, signalling that institutional money has absorbed the liquidity and is ready to move in the opposite direction.

Core concept: A fakeout is not just a wick — it is a wick that sweeps a structurally significant level (prior swing high/low, 4H high/low, daily high/low) and closes back inside within 1–3 bars. The close-back is the confirmation.

Indicator Layers

HTF Bias Engine
Reads Daily and 4H candle structure to establish directional bias. Daily > 4H in priority. Bias determines which side of signals are valid.
Sweep Override
When price wicks through and closes back inside a Daily or 4H level, the bias flips automatically. This is the key intelligence layer — it prevents signals firing in the wrong direction after a major liquidity grab.
Fakeout Detector
Identifies the wick candle (sweep bar) and confirmation candle. Checks body ratio, directional close, and overextension before firing a signal.
Liquidity Lines
Persistent dotted lines showing Daily/4H/1H previous highs, lows, and closes. Colour-coded by bias direction: orange = sell-side, cyan = buy-side, purple = daily close.
Trade Visualiser
Draws entry, SL, TP1, TP2, TP3 levels as coloured horizontal lines with shaded zones and live R:R labels when a signal fires.
Checklist Table
Bottom-right table evaluating 3 conditions in real-time: Liq Sweep ✅, Close Back ✅, Clear Target ✅. Grades the setup: HIGH PROB / MODERATE / LOW PROB.

Recommended Use

Apply the indicator on 5m or 15m charts. The bias engine reads higher timeframes automatically — you do not need to switch charts. Use the Bias Table (top-right) as a constant reference for which direction signals are valid. Only trade signals where all 3 checklist items pass.

Important: This indicator does not provide financial advice. All signals require manual confirmation and risk management. Never enter a trade solely because the indicator fires.

What We're Building
The principles, targets, and success criteria guiding every version of Fakeout Pro.

Design Philosophy

1. Bias-first architecture

Every signal is gated behind the bias engine. An otherwise perfect fakeout pattern that fires against the HTF structure is suppressed. The bias layer is the most important logic in the indicator — signals are secondary.

2. Sweep = context, not just pattern

A wick through a level is meaningless without context. The indicator requires: (a) the level is a real structural reference (prior swing, HTF high/low), (b) the candle has a small body (wick-dominant), and (c) the follow-up candle confirms the rejection. Pattern alone is not enough.

3. Liquidity sweeps change the game

After a major Daily or 4H low is swept, the bias must flip. The market has grabbed the sell-side liquidity — it no longer has a reason to go lower in the immediate term. Continuing to show SELL signals after a Daily Low sweep was the core bug addressed in v5.

4. Clean, actionable visualisation

A trader should see the signal and immediately know: where to enter, where the SL goes, and what the target is. The old arrow + box approach provided none of that. The new entry/SL/TP visualisation makes the trade fully legible at a glance.

Success Criteria

Signal Quality
Less than 20% of fired signals should be clearly wrong in hindsight (wrong direction, no follow-through). Target: 70%+ hit TP1 when all 3 checklist items pass.
Bias Flip Speed
Bias should flip on the same bar that the sweep closes. No lag. No waiting for the next HTF candle (except for reset).
Usability
Any trader who understands liquidity sweeps should be able to use this indicator with zero configuration. Default settings should work on gold, indices, and forex 5m–15m charts.
No Repaint
All signals must be based on confirmed, closed bars only. The use of barmerge.lookahead_off and [1] offsets on HTF data ensures this.
Architecture
System Design
How the indicator's logic layers connect and execute.

Execution Flow

LAYER 1 — HTF DATA FETCH
request.security() × 5 → Daily, 4H, 1H prev bar OHLC
↓
LAYER 2 — BASE BIAS
Daily close position in range → d_bull / d_bear
4H close vs open → h4_bull / h4_bear
Strict mode: requires both | Relaxed: daily only
↓
LAYER 3 — SWEEP OVERRIDE ENGINE ← KEY LAYER
Per-TF sweep state (daily_sweep, h4_sweep, h1_sweep)
Resets on new candle of respective TF
Priority: Daily > 4H > 1H > base bias
Sweep of low → bias_bull=true | Sweep of high → bias_bear=true
↓
LAYER 4 — ATR + OVEREXTENSION
atr(14) → overext_up, overext_dn
Prior move must exceed overext_atr × ATR to qualify
↓
LAYER 5 — SWING STRUCTURE
sw_high = highest(high, swing_len)[str_start]
sw_low = lowest(low, swing_len)[str_start]
sw_doji = body/range ≤ doji_pct
↓
LAYER 6 — SIGNAL CONDITIONS
sell_signal = bear_swept AND sw_doji AND bear_close_back AND bear_dir AND bias_bear AND overext_up
buy_signal = bull_swept AND sw_doji AND bull_close_back AND bull_dir AND bias_bull AND overext_dn
↓
LAYER 7 — VISUALISATION
f_draw_trade() → entry + SL + TP1/2/3 lines + zones
Liquidity lines → persistent, bias-coloured dotted lines
Tables → checklist (bottom-right) + bias (top-right)

HTF Data Strategy

All HTF data uses lookahead_off to prevent future-bar leakage. The [1] offset ensures only the previously closed HTF bar is used for bias — never the currently forming candle.

Key Variables

sweep_bar
= confirm_bars. The offset to the actual sweep candle (the one with the long wick).
str_start
= confirm_bars + 1. The start of the lookback window for swing high/low calculation. Excludes the sweep candle itself.
daily_sweep
Persistent int: 0 = none, 1 = high swept (bear flip), -1 = low swept (bull flip). Resets on new daily bar.
h4_sweep
Same as daily_sweep but for 4H timeframe. Resets on new 4H candle.
is_overridden
Boolean. True when any sweep state is active. Displayed in bias table as "OVR".

Signal Logic
How Signals Fire
Exact conditions required for a buy or sell fakeout signal to trigger.

BUY Signal — All Conditions Required

  • SWING Sweep candle's LOW went BELOW the prior swing low (highest(high, swing_len)[str_start]). The wick broke structure downward.
  • DOJI Sweep candle body ÷ range ≤ doji_pct. Candle is wick-dominated — small body = rejection, not trend.
  • CLOSE Confirmation candle closes ABOVE the swept swing low — price returned inside the range.
  • DIR Confirmation candle is bullish: close > open.
  • BIAS bias_bull = true. Either daily closed bullish, or a Daily/4H low was recently swept (override active).
  • EXT Prior move was a drop of at least overext_atr × ATR — confirms price was extended before the reversal.

    • SELL Signal — All Conditions Required

  • SWING Sweep candle's HIGH went ABOVE the prior swing high. Wick broke structure upward.
  • DOJI Sweep candle body ÷ range ≤ doji_pct.
  • CLOSE Confirmation candle closes BELOW the swept swing high.
  • DIR Confirmation candle is bearish: close < open.
  • BIAS bias_bear = true.
  • EXT Prior move was a rally of at least overext_atr × ATR.

    • Sweep Override Logic

    New in v5: When ANY of the following occurs on the current bar, bias flips immediately — overriding the base Daily bias.
  • DAILY Daily Low Sweep: low < d_l AND close > d_l → bias flips BULL. Resets on next daily open.
  • DAILY Daily High Sweep: high > d_h AND close < d_h → bias flips BEAR. Resets on next daily open.
  • 4H 4H Low Sweep: low < h4_l AND close > h4_l → bias flips BULL. Resets on next 4H open.
  • 4H 4H High Sweep: high > h4_h AND close < h4_h → bias flips BEAR. Resets on next 4H open.

    • Checklist Evaluation

    Three boolean conditions are evaluated independently and displayed in the checklist table:

    Liq Sweep
    Did the sweep candle's wick actually break the structural level? (high[sweep_bar] > sw_high for sells, low[sweep_bar] < sw_low for buys)
    Close Back
    Did the confirmation candle close back inside the swept range? (close < sw_high AND close > sw_low for sells, vice versa for buys)
    Clear Target
    Is there a meaningful HTF liquidity level (Daily/4H/1H) at least 0.8× ATR away in the trade direction?
    Glossary
    Terms & Definitions
    Trading concepts and internal terminology used throughout this indicator and documentation.

    Trading Concepts

    Liquidity
    Clusters of stop orders sitting above swing highs (buy-stops) or below swing lows (sell-stops). Institutional players use these pools to fill large orders.
    Liquidity Sweep
    When price moves through a key level, triggers the stop orders (collects the liquidity), then reverses. Often appears as a long wick candle.
    Fakeout
    A failed breakout. Price breaks a level suggesting a move in one direction, then rapidly reverses. The sweep is the "fake" move; the close-back is the signal.
    HTF Bias
    Higher Timeframe Bias. The directional context from daily and 4-hour charts. Trading in the direction of HTF bias significantly improves win rate.
    Overextension
    When price has moved a significant distance in one direction (measured by ATR multiples) without a pullback. Overextended moves are more likely to reverse sharply.
    Morning Star
    3-candle bullish reversal: large bearish candle → small doji/spinning top (sweep low) → large bullish confirmation. The sweep version requires the doji wick to break a structural low.
    Evening Star
    3-candle bearish reversal: large bullish candle → small doji (sweep high) → large bearish confirmation. Sweep version requires wick above structural high.
    Sell-side Liq
    Buy-stop orders sitting above swing highs. Called "sell-side" because institutional sellers use these levels to sell into the buy orders.
    Buy-side Liq
    Sell-stop orders sitting below swing lows. Institutions buy into these sell orders.
    R:R
    Risk-to-Reward ratio. A 1:2R trade risks 1 unit to gain 2. The indicator shows live R:R for each TP level.

    Internal Terminology

    sweep_bar
    The bar offset of the actual sweep candle (the wick candle). Equal to confirm_bars.
    str_start
    The bar offset where the swing structure lookback begins. Equal to confirm_bars + 1.
    sw_high / sw_low
    The structural swing high or low that price needs to breach for a valid sweep.
    sw_doji
    Boolean. True when the sweep candle has a body ≤ doji_pct of its full range.
    bias_bull / bias_bear
    Final bias booleans after base bias and sweep override are both applied.
    is_overridden
    True when a sweep override is currently active (any TF sweep state ≠ 0).

    Usage Guide
    How to Use Fakeout Pro
    Step-by-step workflow for identifying and executing fakeout setups.

    Step 1 — Check the Bias Table

    Before looking at any signals, check the top-right bias table. You need to know: what direction is the indicator biased? If it shows ⬇ BEAR, only trade sell signals. If it shows ⬆ BULL, only trade buy signals. If it shows ⚡ OVR, a major level was just swept — the bias has flipped.

    Step 2 — Identify the Liquidity Lines

    The dotted lines show key levels price is likely to target. In a bear bias, orange lines above price = sell-side liquidity (targets for price to sweep before reversing lower). In a bull bias, cyan lines below = buy-side liquidity.

    Setup condition: A signal is most valuable when it fires AT one of the liquidity lines — meaning price swept the line, closed back, and the indicator fired. This is the classic "liquidity sweep → fakeout entry" sequence.

    Step 3 — Wait for the Signal

    When a ▲ BUY FAKEOUT or ▼ SELL FAKEOUT appears, the indicator draws the full trade visual: entry line, SL zone, and TP levels. The sweep candle is marked with a small ⚡.

    Step 4 — Check the Checklist

    Bottom-right table shows 3 conditions. Only take trades where all 3 are ✅ (HIGH PROB). If 1–2 pass, treat as a lower-confidence setup and reduce size. If none pass, skip the trade.

    Step 5 — Execute

    Entry
    At the yellow entry line (signal candle close). Can also enter on the next candle open for a safer fill.
    Stop Loss
    At the red SL line. This is placed beyond the sweep wick extreme with a small buffer.
    TP1
    Take partial profit (50%) at TP1. Move SL to breakeven.
    TP2
    Take remaining position at TP2, or hold for TP3 if momentum is strong.

    Design Decisions
    Why We Built It This Way
    Architectural and design decisions with rationale. Helps future development stay consistent with original intent.

    Decision Log

    DD-001 · Use lookahead_off for all HTF data

    Decision: All request.security() calls use barmerge.lookahead_off and reference [1] offsets (previous bar).

    Rationale: lookahead_on with current bar data causes the indicator to repaint — signals appear and disappear as the live bar develops. This destroys trust and backtesting validity. Non-repainting is a non-negotiable requirement.

    Trade-off: Signals fire 1 bar later than they theoretically could. Accepted — accuracy beats speed.

    DD-002 · Bias override priority: Daily > 4H > 1H

    Decision: When multiple sweep states are active, Daily takes priority over 4H over 1H.

    Rationale: A Daily sweep is a larger, more significant liquidity event than a 4H sweep. If both are active, the Daily context should dominate. 1H sweeps are the least reliable and are off by default.

    DD-003 · Sweep state resets on new TF candle, not on signal fire

    Decision: h4_sweep resets to 0 when a new 4H candle opens, not when a fakeout signal fires.

    Rationale: The sweep context is relevant for the entire duration of the current 4H candle — multiple LTF fakeout entries may be valid within that window. Resetting on signal would suppress valid second entries.

    DD-004 · TP calculated as multiples of SL distance, not ATR

    Decision: TP distances are risk × multiplier where risk = entry - SL. Not ATR × multiplier.

    Rationale: R:R ratios should be consistent regardless of ATR. If ATR is used for both SL and TP, a larger ATR produces both a wider SL and further TP — which maintains R:R. But using ATR directly for TP when SL is set to wick extreme creates inconsistent R:R. Risk-based TP is cleaner.

    DD-005 · Swing structure excludes the sweep candle from lookback

    Decision: sw_high = ta.highest(high, swing_len)[str_start] where str_start = confirm_bars + 1. The sweep candle itself is not included in the structural high/low calculation.

    Rationale: Including the sweep candle in the lookback would mean it could set its own high as the swing level — defeating the purpose. The structural level must predate the sweep candle.

    Developer Notes
    Working Notes
    Observations, experiments, and ideas captured during development sessions.

    Session Notes

    May 2026 — v5 Development

    The core insight for v5 was that the sweep override in v4 only ever watched Daily levels. The 4H low sweep scenario (which is the most common intraday reversal trigger, especially on XAUUSD) was completely ignored. The fix was straightforward once identified — just three more var int h4_sweep state checks mirroring the daily logic.

    The trade visualisation overhaul was also important. The old "sweep box + arrow" was genuinely confusing — it highlighted the wrong candle, the box looked like a bearish body, and there was no indication of where to enter or exit. The new entry/SL/TP ghost layout communicates the entire trade plan at a glance.

    One Pine v6 quirk: the f_upsert_line() function returning a tuple [line, label] had to be carefully typed. Type inference sometimes fails on tuple returns from functions called conditionally — watch out for this in future refactors.

    May 2026 — v3 Session Notes

    Scrapped the textbook morning/evening star pattern entirely. The problem was that a "textbook" evening star requires candle[-2] to be a large bullish body — but in real setups, the move before the sweep is often multiple candles, not one single large candle. The indicator was completely missing valid setups because of this rigid check.

    Replacing with "wick broke structure + closed back + directional confirm" immediately caught the setups that were being missed. The overextension check replaces the "large body" requirement — if the move prior to the sweep was significant (1.2× ATR or more), we don't care about the shape of any single candle in that move.

    Observations from Live Testing

  • OBSGold (XAUUSD) on 5m is the most reliable asset for this strategy. Daily sweeps of 4H lows happen frequently and the reversal is often sharp enough to hit TP1 within 2–4 hours.
  • OBSThe "HIGH PROB" grade (all 3 checklist items passing) appears to correlate with cleaner entries. MODERATE setups (2/3 pass) have more follow-through variability.
  • OBSOverextension filter (overext_atr) is the most impactful parameter for reducing false signals in chop. 1.5 significantly cleaner than 1.0 in ranging markets.
  • OBS1H sweep override is off by default for good reason — it fires too frequently on 5m charts and creates bias confusion.
  • OBSSignals during the Asian session (no London/NY overlap) tend to have lower follow-through. Session filter is a priority for v6.
